Directional asymmetry of the ambient field in the body-wave fan
  This is Lellouch et al. (2019)'s own diagnostic for surface
  illumination: downgoing energy strongly exceeding upgoing.
  Reported as |A| only -- convention-free, see module docstring.
  Fan 2500-4000 m/s, positive frequencies only.

=== 5-12 Hz ===
  2017 pre-event   |A| = 0.0007  (null 95th 0.2871, p = 0.9975)  fan holds 0.32% of in-band energy over 26 cells
  2024-25 ambient  |A| = 0.0001  (null 95th 0.9983, p = 0.9726)  fan holds 30.77% of in-band energy over 26 cells

=== 12-20 Hz ===
  2017 pre-event   |A| = 0.4158  (null 95th 0.2687, p = 0.0025)  fan holds 1.15% of in-band energy over 56 cells
  2024-25 ambient  |A| = 0.0009  (null 95th 0.4146, p = 0.9975)  fan holds 0.42% of in-band energy over 56 cells

=== interpretation ===
  2017 pre-event   significant asymmetry in 1 of 2 bands
  2024-25 ambient  significant asymmetry in 0 of 2 bands

  THE MISSING INGREDIENT. The 2017 pre-event noise carries a
  significant directional preference in the body-wave fan and the
  2024-25 ambient field does not. Lellouch's inference of surface
  sources rests on exactly that asymmetry, so this says the
  2024-25 field is NOT illuminated from the surface in this band.
  A downgoing P cannot be recovered from a field that has no net
  downgoing energy: no filter can create a propagation direction
  that is not in the data. This is consistent with Behm (2016),
  where 30 s sufficed under good illumination, and with our stacks
  getting WORSE with more data (96 h coherent stack p = 0.9184).

  LIMITS: 2017 is ~5 s of pre-event noise from two records, so its
  estimate is noisy; the fan is the frozen 2500-4000 m/s selection; and
  |A| measures a preferred direction ALONG THE FIBRE, which in a near-
  vertical borehole is close to but not exactly vertical.
