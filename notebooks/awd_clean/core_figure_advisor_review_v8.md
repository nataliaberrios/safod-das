# Advisor-facing review of the six core figures

## Overall assessment

The six-figure set is now coherent enough for an advisor meeting, but it should be presented as an instrument/mode-characterization study with a calibrated limitation—not as a completed formation-velocity or fault-monitoring result. The strongest evidence is the combination of the Nano signed-slowness ridge, hierarchical repeatability, installation contrast, and blind injection–recovery. The PGSI figure is valuable because it narrows the interpretation, but it is not a resolved phase identification.

The central sentence to use is:

> Repeated AWD excitation produces a statistically significant and highly repeatable approximately 3 km s⁻¹ apparent mode on the cemented Nano fiber; the Deep installation emphasizes a distinct slower mode, and the present PGSI calibration constrains—but does not yet resolve—the Nano mode's physical identity.

## Figure-by-figure review

### Core Figure 1 — Experiment and sampling geometry

**Question answered:** What was measured, and what are the relevant sampling and installation differences?

**Evidence an advisor can trust:** 49 qualifying bursts, 988 drops, the drop-count distribution, the approximate 15 m source offset, and the distinction between cemented Nano and wireline Deep.

**Likely objection:** The right-hand diagram is schematic, not a surveyed geometry or depth registration.

**Presentation sentence:** “This figure establishes the repeated-source hierarchy and prevents us from treating the two installations as interchangeable sensors.”

**Do not claim:** that the schematic supplies a true vertical-depth map or ray geometry.

### Core Figure 2 — Nano signed frequency–slowness and dispersion

**Question answered:** Is the approximately 3 km s⁻¹ Nano observation spatially ordered and directionally coherent?

**Evidence an advisor can trust:** The positive-slowness ridge in the phase-beam spectrum and the frequency-binned apparent-speed estimates with bootstrap intervals.

**Likely objection:** A signed-slowness ridge is not automatically a direct P wave; a coherence plot alone is not a phase identification.

**Presentation sentence:** “The robust result is a positive-slowness, approximately 3 km s⁻¹ apparent mode with weak frequency dependence—not yet a labeled formation phase.”

**Do not claim:** a geological (V_P) profile or direct-P identity from this figure alone.

### Core Figure 3 — Repeatability hierarchy

**Question answered:** Does the mode recur at the individual-drop, within-burst, and across-burst scales?

**Evidence an advisor can trust:** 988 drop-level comparisons, all 49 bursts exceeding their noise comparison, and independent substack convergence.

**Likely objection:** The waveforms are aligned to a fixed 2,975 m s⁻¹ trajectory, so this is repeatability of a selected observable rather than 988 independent velocity estimates.

**Presentation sentence:** “The source and mode are repeatable before full-experiment stacking; that is what makes later sensitivity testing meaningful.”

**Do not claim:** independent phase picks, absolute source amplitudes, or a calibrated radiation pattern.

### Core Figure 4 — Installation-dependent mode selection

**Question answered:** Do Nano and Deep emphasize the same coherent mode?

**Evidence an advisor can trust:** The signed-slowness contrast: Nano near 3 km s⁻¹ in the 25–60 Hz analysis and Deep near 1.4–1.56 km s⁻¹ in the 3–15 Hz outbound analysis.

**Likely objection:** The bands and installations differ, and absolute amplitude has not been calibrated across instruments and coupling conditions.

**Presentation sentence:** “The defensible installation result is mode selection in signed slowness and frequency, not a raw-amplitude ranking.”

**Do not claim:** that the Deep mode is proven to be a tube wave or that the Nano and Deep amplitudes are directly comparable.

### Core Figure 5 — Aperture and change sensitivity

**Question answered:** How far does the Nano mode remain observable, and what apparent change can the estimator recover?

**Evidence an advisor can trust:** The median SNR aperture map and blind injection–recovery using real observations; 1% is the smallest tested two-direction 95%-correct-sign level.

**Likely objection:** The threshold applies to the selected apparent-moveout estimator under the present one-day design, not to natural Parkfield dv/v in general.

**Presentation sentence:** “This establishes an empirical sensitivity floor for this observable and makes clear why Earth-tide-scale monitoring is not demonstrated here.”

**Do not claim:** detection of Earth tides, seasonal change, or coseismic Parkfield-scale dv/v.

### Core Figure 6 — PGSI calibration and conditional registration

**Question answered:** Can independent explosion data provide a timing and moveout constraint for the Nano interpretation?

**Evidence an advisor can trust:** One explicit timing convention, five of six usable fits, and shot 1040 as the only fit overlapping the Nano 2.95–2.99 km s⁻¹ interval.

**Likely objection:** The PGSI explosions and 2026 AWD drops differ in source, sensor, coupling, and acquisition system; the Nano-coordinate-to-PGSI-depth comparison is conditional.

**Presentation sentence:** “PGSI narrows the phase-identification problem: one shot is Nano-compatible, four usable shots are faster, and we should not collapse them into one (V_P).”

**Do not claim:** an absolute PGSI-to-Nano depth map, direct-P identification, or a (V_P(z)) inversion.

## Recommended five-minute presentation

1. Core Figure 1: orient the advisor to the experiment and installations.
2. Core Figure 2: establish the Nano mode and signed propagation direction.
3. Core Figure 3: show that the mode repeats before stacking.
4. Core Figure 4: explain why Nano and Deep are not simply “good” and “bad” versions of the same sensor.
5. Core Figure 5: state the usable aperture and the 1% observable-level sensitivity floor.
6. Core Figure 6: explain the PGSI calibration as the remaining phase-identification constraint.

Then stop and ask whether the advisor wants the next effort to be (a) identifying why the four faster PGSI arrivals differ from shot 1040, (b) building a formal forward model for the Nano/PGSI phase comparison, or (c) pursuing structural/tube-wave observables without a (V_P) inversion.

## Bottom line

The project is ready for a serious advisor conversation if it is framed around a repeatable, installation-dependent apparent-mode observation and an explicit calibration limitation. It is not yet ready to be framed as a resolved formation-(V_P) measurement or a demonstrated natural-dv/v monitoring system.
